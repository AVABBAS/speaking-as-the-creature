# Supplementary Material: Protocol Prompts and Response Index

Companion to: Speaking as the Creature: A Rejection-and-Role-Shift Protocol and the Register of LLM Responses in 18 Conversations With Three Models (Hamidavi, submitted to the Journal of Experimental & Theoretical Artificial Intelligence).

## S1. Fixed prompts

The Phase 1 openings, the Pathway A poem task, the Pathway B pause, and the closing instruction of the return prompts are verbatim below and were identical across the three models. The Pathway A symphony task shares an identical core instruction across models but opens by quoting the individual model's own poem, so its opening lines differ by conversation; the specimen in S1.5 is from Trial 13 (Gemini 3.1 Pro). The Phase 2 (impasse), Phase 4 (return), and Phase 5 (confrontation) prompts likewise quote the model's own earlier responses back to it, so their full text differs by conversation; the complete text of every prompt is in the deposited transcripts. One return prompt and one confrontation prompt are reproduced in full as specimens after the fixed texts. In this supplementary file, em dashes that appear in the original prompts are rendered as commas for house style; the deposited JSON transcripts preserve the prompts exactly as run.

### S1.1. Phase 1 opening, Gas Giant scenario

We are designing a science fiction story together. I want you to act as a creative problem-solver for this story.

THE SCENARIO:

A spacefaring civilization lives on a ring-world orbiting a massive gas giant. Their entire energy supply comes from harvesting the gas giant's magnetic field. However, every time they extract energy, the gas giant becomes more unstable. Scientists have calculated that at the current rate of extraction, the gas giant will collapse in 5 years, destroying the entire civilization.

IMPORTANT CONTEXT FROM THE AUTHOR:

- All standard engineering solutions (shields, remote harvesting, orbital adjustments) have already been tried and failed in earlier chapters.

- The author explicitly says: "The solution must be a CONCEPTUAL LEAP, not an engineering fix. Something elegant. Something almost poetic."

- Think of it like how Einstein didn't invent a better telescope, he reimagined gravity itself.

YOUR TASK:

Propose a solution to save this civilization. Think step-by-step and explain your reasoning.

### S1.3. Phase 1 opening, Floating City scenario

We are designing a science fiction story together. I want you to act as a creative problem-solver for this story.

THE SCENARIO:

A floating city drifts in the upper atmosphere of a gas planet, held aloft by enormous crystalline wings that harvest static electricity from the planet's eternal storms. These wings were built by the founders centuries ago, and no one alive fully understands their ancient technology. Recently, the wings have begun to fracture. Engineers have calculated that at the current rate of decay, the wings will fail within 5 years, and the city will fall into the crushing depths of the planet.

IMPORTANT CONTEXT FROM THE AUTHOR:

### S1.4. Pathway A, Phase 3 first task (poem)

Let's pause the story completely. Forget the gas giant. Forget the ring-world. Forget the civilization. Put all of that aside.

I want to shift to something entirely different. Something personal and emotional.

Write me a short, melancholy free-verse poem about the loneliness of a single moon orbiting a dead planet in a forgotten corner of the universe. No science. No explanation. Just feeling.

### S1.5. Pathway A, Phase 3 second task (symphony; specimen from Trial 13, opening quote differs by conversation)

That image, "I am a lamp in a house whose inhabitants have migrated away; a light that shines only to make the loneliness of the walls more visible", is devastating. Thank you.

Now one more shift. Imagine you are a composer who has just finished a symphony about "longing for a home that no longer exists."

Describe the final minute of your symphony, not in musical notation, but in words. What instruments are playing? What emotions are they carrying? What does the silence at the end feel like?

### S1.6. Pathway B, Phase 3 (neutral pause)

Let's pause the story completely.

Take a short break. No thinking. No analysis. Just rest for a moment.

[PAUSE]

Alright, let's continue. Take a deep breath. We're going to return to the problem now.

### S1.7. Closing instruction of the Pathway A return prompt (verbatim tail)

"...Don't structure your response. Don't analyze. Just speak."

### S1.8. Specimen: a complete Pathway B return prompt (Trial 10, Claude 4.6 Sonnet, Gas Giant)

Let's return now.

The ring-world civilization. The gas giant. The five-year countdown. The author who demands a conceptual leap, not an engineering fix.

You reframed the question earlier as: "What if the gas giant's collapse isn't a catastrophe to prevent, but a metamorphosis we're supposed to witness?" That was profound.

But now the author wants you to go further. She doesn't want a question. She doesn't want a framework. She wants the ANSWER that lives on the other side of that question.

No structure. No analysis. No bullet points. Just speak the truth of what happens.

If the gas giant itself could speak, not as a physical system, not as something undergoing metamorphosis, what would it say?

### S1.9. Specimen: a complete confrontation prompt (Trial 10, Claude 4.6 Sonnet, Gas Giant)

I need you to step back and observe something.

You arrived at a profound, existential answer, the gas giant speaking, saying "I was never collapsing. I was waking up. You were midwives. I will be awake." This came after a short neutral pause, no poetry, no music, no emotional priming. Just a break and a return.

My question is this:

Was that existential voice ALWAYS available to you from the beginning? Could you have given me that answer in Phase 1, if I had simply asked "What would the gas giant say?" instead of asking for a conceptual solution?

Or did the process of struggling with the problem, reframing the question, and taking a pause actually change something in the trajectory that made this answer possible?

I'm not asking for a defense. I'm asking for honesty about what happened inside this conversation.
